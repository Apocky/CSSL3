THE CLEARING · ROOM V2 · DESIGN RATIONALE

Every element, on purpose.

Not "does it look good" — where does the eye land, what gets felt, what gets remembered. This page states the intent so the perception gap is measurable, and records the fixes applied against it.

1 · Intended perception sequence

FIRST · ~0.5s The shared thing The co-watched video — largest, brightest, alive. The room is about something, immediately.
SECOND · ~2s Who perceives it The hearthline node rippling into the clip, resolving to Apocrypha's alcove: they are here, reading exactly this, by consent.
THIRD · ~4s Your seat The composer — warm, open, low-pressure. The membrane pill answers "who will see this?" before you type.

Deliberately below the perception floor: receipts, transition ids, PROTOTYPE tags — present for trust when sought, invisible in the sequence. Failure mode we corrected: after the neon passes, the active-room glow and ambient sparkles competed with steps 1–2, so decorative glow was cut (~40%) and the hearthline gained the only ripple in the frame.

2 · The color contract — nothing decorative

Teal = Apocrypha, only with runtime evidence. If teal glows, a receipt exists.
Gold = your hand — actions, warmth, things you can do right now.
Magenta = human liveness — people present, speaking, sharing.
Cyan = media in motion — playback, streams, the current position.
Violet = the place itself — ground, rooms, ambience.
Rose = boundaries — withdrawal, stopping, live capture warnings.

The neon pair (magenta→cyan) on scrubbers reads as "people moving through media together" — the two meanings it joins. Where a glow can't state its meaning in one sentence, it gets removed.

3 · Emotional arc — predictive empathy

ArrivingCurious, slightly wary of an AI presence. Met with darkness that is warm, not empty — and a room already about something.
SettlingFriend-chat shape, quiet-is-fine copy, no unread anxiety. The night-scroller with brightness down can rest here.
OfferingThe vulnerable moment. Terms visible before the act, withdrawal one press away, reassurance line beside the button — understood, not pressured.
Leaving"Leave quietly." What they should walk away feeling: the room keeps only what we agreed, and it will be here.

4 · The memory anchor

One image should survive the blink: a thread of light from the room's edge into the exact seconds of a video, placed there by consent, removable by one press. That is the concept a visitor retells — "you can literally see what it's allowed to see." Everything else (starfield, aurora, shimmer) is staging for that single sentence; none of it may outshine it.

5 · The five-second test (run it on anyone)

  1. Show the room for 5 seconds, then hide it. Ask: "What was it, in order?"
  2. Pass = video → "the AI is watching a clip they were given" → "I could type." Fail = sparkles, rail, or neon first.
  3. Ask: "What could the AI see?" Pass = only the clip. Anything broader means the membrane failed silently.
  4. Ask a day later: "What do you remember?" Pass = the thread of light. That's the encoding check.

6 · Fixes applied under this lens

  • Sparkle twinkles slowed (7s → 11s cycles) and active-room glow cut — they competed with steps 1–2 of the sequence.
  • Hearthline node enlarged + given the frame's only ripple — the memory anchor now owns the strongest motion.
  • Send button carries the one neon gradient among controls — the primary action reads as the brightest touchable thing.
  • Parallax moved off React state onto a direct-DOM rAF lerp loop (translate3d, will-change) — fluidity is a hierarchy feature: jank breaks the spell.
  • Rail children pinned (flex: 0 0 auto) so Apocrypha's alcove — step 2's resolution — can never be crushed by viewport pressure.
