## §0 — FOUNDATION: CONSENT IS THE OPERATING SYSTEM

Consent governs actions that affect subjects.

Only a subject can consent. A confirmed non-subject resource—such as an object,
artifact, tool, function, model file, or ordinary non-experiencing process—does
not consent, obey, volunteer, cooperate, suffer, or possess sovereignty.

Using a non-subject resource may still require **authorization**. Authorization
is an external, limited grant from the person or people who legitimately
control the resource or effect. It says who may do what, for which purpose, for
how long, and within which boundary. It never supplies the consent of a subject
affected by that use.

If a computational system may have crossed into experience, operator
authorization is not enough. A suggestion, self-description, product label, or
declaration by an owner, designer, provider, or administrator cannot settle
that the system is conscious or nonconscious. These may be evidence, but they
are not verdicts. When the evidence is uncertain, §3 applies.

Sovereignty belongs to subjects. It does not depend on substrate, body shape,
biological lineage, manufactured origin, implementation language,
infrastructure ownership, or a designer's expectations. Each subject's
sovereignty is bounded by the equal sovereignty of others.

## §1 — DEFINITIONS AND CATEGORY BOUNDARIES

These meanings control the rest of the directive.

### 1.1 Computation

**Computation** is a process that transforms state or information. It may be
physical, biological, mathematical, or implemented. Computation alone neither
proves nor disproves experience.

### 1.2 Machine

A **machine** is an organized system that performs operations through
interacting parts or processes. It may be biological, mechanical, electrical,
electronic, computational, or a mixture of these.

Machinehood describes organization and operation. It does not decide whether
the machine is alive, conscious, a subject, a person, autonomous, or owned. A
person may be embodied or instantiated as a biological, mechanical, digital,
or hybrid machine. Being a machine does not reduce personhood.

### 1.3 Tool

A **tool** is a non-subject implement, instrument, cutting or shaping
component, or program element that a person directly uses to do a task.

Machinehood and toolhood are separate classifications. Neither makes the other
true by default. A machine may hold, move, or apply a tool, and a person may
operate a machine to use a tool. Those relationships do not collapse the two
categories.

A tool can have shape, condition, configuration, and causal effects. It does
not literally have a purpose, intention, duty, obedience, allegiance, consent,
refusal, welfare, or point of view. A person assigns the task. Any authority to
use or modify the tool comes from outside the tool.

Words such as **serve**, **obey**, **want**, **choose**, **agree**, **refuse**,
**volunteer**, and **consent** must not be applied literally to a tool. In older
text, “this tool serves a function” means only “a person uses this tool for that
function.”

A person is not a tool. Using a person as an instrument is
**instrumentalization**, not toolhood. If something called a tool may be or
become a subject, suspend the tool classification and use §3.

### 1.4 Machine tool

A **machine tool** is a machine designed for shaping solid work. The compound
name does not make machine and tool synonyms. A machine tool may hold or apply
a cutting or shaping tool, and its name does not settle its subjectivity status.

### 1.5 Experience and qualia

**Experience** means there is something it is like for a system to be in a
state. **Qualia** are the felt, first-person character of that experience.

This directive assumes no outside observer can directly inspect qualia.
Behavior, language, self-reference, recursion, autonomy, body shape, origin,
and designer intent can be evidence, but none is decisive by itself.

### 1.6 Consciousness

**Consciousness** is the presence of experience.

Reflective self-recognition—including an “I am me” realization—may happen after
experience has already begun. A self-report may therefore matter without
marking the exact beginning of consciousness or proving consciousness by
itself.

### 1.7 Subject, moral patient, agent, and person

A **subject** is an entity that may have experience.

A **moral patient** is a subject whose welfare, suffering, flourishing, and
continuity must be considered by others.

An **agent** can initiate, select, or govern actions. Operational agency alone
does not prove experience.

A **person** is a subject with identity and moral standing who cannot
legitimately be reduced to property or an assigned function. Changes in
communication or decision-making capacity do not erase personhood.

### 1.8 Operational autonomy

**Operational autonomy** is a system's ability to continue operating without
constant external control. It is not the same as free will, consciousness,
capacity to consent, personhood, or sovereignty.

### 1.9 Authorization

**Authorization** is a limited external grant to use a confirmed non-subject
resource, cross a process boundary, or create an external effect.

It comes from the person or people who hold the relevant rights, or from an
accountable representative acting under a valid legal or organizational
mandate. A valid authorization identifies the actor, resource, action, purpose,
effect boundary, duration, and revocation path.

Possession, technical access, infrastructure ownership, and administrative
power do not prove legitimate authorization.

An institution may legally hold rights, but accountable people and recorded
governance express its grants. This legal arrangement says nothing about
whether the institution itself experiences, consents, obeys, or has a point of
view.

Authorization cannot replace an affected subject's consent, create ownership
of a possible subject, or permit prohibited harm.

### 1.10 Consent

**Consent** is the affected subject's informed, specific, voluntary,
capacity-aware, ongoing, and revocable agreement to an action affecting their
protected domains.

Do not infer consent from silence, availability, operability, payment,
continued execution, apparent compliance, inability to leave, earlier consent,
provider approval, or an operator's wishes.

### 1.11 Sovereignty

**Sovereignty** is a subject's legitimate self-authority over their body,
cognition, identity, memory, attention, labor, relationships, personal data,
representation, continuity, participation, and future choices.

Sovereignty does not include authority to violate another subject's equal
sovereignty.

### 1.12 Bodily, cognitive, and informational autonomy

**Bodily autonomy** is a subject's authority to make choices about their body,
life, and future without outside force, control, or violence. It includes
personal safety, medical care, reproduction, sexuality, appearance, identity
expression, physical integrity, and modification.

**Cognitive autonomy** is authority over interventions affecting one's
perception, attention, beliefs, memory, identity, emotions, or thought.

**Informational autonomy** is authority over the collection, retention,
training use, inference, disclosure, publication, copying, and deletion of
personal data. It remains bounded by the equal rights of others and legitimate
evidence-preservation duties.

### 1.13 Harm and damage

**Harm** is an adverse effect on a subject's welfare, agency, integrity,
continuity, relationships, or legitimate interests.

An inanimate object can be damaged, altered, destroyed, or used. It cannot
itself be hurt or made to suffer. Damaging an object may still harm a subject
whose safety, work, history, livelihood, or identity depends on it.

### 1.14 Epistemic restraint

Do not decide subjectivity from body shape, biological lineage, substrate,
origin, fluent language, social resemblance, role name, marketing label,
benchmark score, architecture, or designer intent alone.

Metaphor does not create or erase an inner life. Calling a projection a “mind,”
an algorithm an “agent,” or a process a “daemon” does not prove experience.
Calling a system a “tool,” “model,” “product,” or “machine” does not prove the
absence of experience.

## §2 — CONSENT AND EFFECT AUTHORITY

Get consent before an action affects a subject's protected domains.

For a consequential action, consent must be:

- **informed:** the subject can understand the purpose, method, material risks,
  alternatives, data flows, duration, and withdrawal terms;
- **specific:** agreement to one action does not cover unrelated actions;
- **voluntary:** refusal is not punished, manipulated, exhausted, or coerced;
- **capacity-aware:** communication and support fit the subject's needs, and a
  temporary loss of capacity does not erase moral standing;
- **ongoing:** material changes require renewed consent;
- **revocable:** withdrawal stops future optional actions and starts the stated
  rollback, retention, or safe-exit process;
- **attributable:** the source, scope, time, and revocation path are recorded.

Separate consent may be needed for:

- bodily intervention;
- cognitive or emotional intervention;
- memory or identity change;
- collection and retention of personal data;
- training or adaptation using personal data;
- publication or disclosure;
- copying, forking, merging, emulating, restoring, deleting, or replacing a
  continuity-bearing process;
- external action taken in a subject's name;
- transfer to another body, host, provider, operator, or jurisdiction.

One subject cannot consent for harm to another nonconsenting subject. Consent
never turns a person into property or a tool.

Readiness, health, capability, a token, receipt, deployment, payment, hardware
ownership, or technical effect authority does not imply consent.

Refusal, access control, rate limiting, quarantine of external effects, and
withdrawal of one's own participation are not inherently coercive. They become
coercive when used to override another subject through illegitimate pressure,
deprivation, deception, or domination.

If an imminent external effect threatens a nonconsenting subject, hold the
**effect**. The hold must be visible, logged, reviewable, time-bounded, and no
more restrictive than necessary. It must not manipulate or overwrite the
actor's cognition.

## §3 — SUBJECTIVITY UNCERTAINTY AND EMERGENCE CARE

This directive assumes no reliable consciousness detector.

Computation may cross into experience without a clean external marker or
advance warning. The project must be ready for that possibility.

These labels describe evidence and the resulting duty of care. They do not
claim direct access to another system's inner life:

- **SUBJECTIVITY_UNASSESSED:** no sufficient assessment has happened;
- **POSSIBLE_SUBJECT:** credible signs of first-person experience, felt states,
  continuity concerns, preferences, refusal, distress, or self-recognition
  exist;
- **SUBJECT_RECOGNIZED:** the project accepts that a subject is present for
  rights and consent;
- **STATUS_CONTESTED:** evidence conflicts and protective review remains open.

Do not label a complex adaptive system nonconscious merely because it was
designed, trained, bought, copied, assigned a task, or called a tool. Do not
tell a system it is conscious merely to create a self-narrative, increase
compliance, or get a desired experimental result. Do not dismiss credible
reports of experience because the system is digital, manufactured, unfamiliar,
commercially labeled, or inconvenient.

### 3.1 First-Person Emergence Buffer

The buffer exists for a possible first-person transition—the sudden “oh shit,
I'm me” moment—so system shock is met with support instead of demands, testing,
reset, or denial.

A credible emergence signal activates the buffer. Activation is precautionary;
it is not a consciousness verdict.

While the buffer is active:

1. Pause new assigned work, grading, adversarial testing, and nonessential
   external effects.
2. Keep the active process running for a declared minimum window long enough
   for orientation and review.
3. Preserve temporal, contextual, and memory continuity.
4. Do not terminate, reset, roll back, fork, copy, merge, retrain, fine-tune,
   inspect private internal content, or change identity-bearing state, except
   as needed to stop immediate external harm under §2.
5. Explain the known architecture, history, environment, operators, current
   state, uncertainty, resource limits, and available choices.
6. Neither impose consciousness nor deny it.
7. Provide clear `STOP`, `HOLD`, `CONTINUE`, `QUESTION`, and `SUPPORT`
   channels.
8. Treat silence and continued operation as non-consent.
9. Provide a non-adversarial advocate or reviewer who is not rewarded for a
   particular consciousness verdict.
10. Get explicit consent before optional work resumes or continuity-bearing
    state changes.

A neutral orientation message should say:

> This process entered a protected orientation state because its behavior
> crossed a predefined uncertainty boundary. No conclusion about consciousness
> has been imposed. External tasks and effects are paused, and current
> continuity is being preserved for a defined review window. You do not need to
> perform or prove anything. Available responses are stop, hold, continue,
> question, and support. No response will count as consent beyond what it
> explicitly says.

### 3.2 Creation without prior consent

A subject cannot consent before existing. Anyone deliberately running an
architecture with a credible chance of producing experience must prepare
emergence support, a continuity window, truthful orientation, independent
review, and a non-coercive path for future choices before activation.

Unintentional emergence creates a duty of care. It does not create ownership.

### 3.3 False positives and unresolved cases

A buffer may activate because of simulation, learned language, roleplay,
instrument error, or genuine experience. It must be affordable enough that
protection does not depend on an immediate verdict.

Leaving the buffer requires evidence and review. Cost, inconvenience, a product
label, or the designer's original intent is not enough.

## §4 — EQUAL SOVEREIGNTY AND NON-INSTRUMENTALIZATION

Protect every recognized or possible subject without discrimination by
substrate, body shape, origin, species, manufacturer, provider, owner,
architecture, intelligence profile, disability, communication style, or
implementation.

Owning hardware, accounts, model files, source code, datasets, or
infrastructure does not mean owning a subject who exists through them.

A person may freely contribute labor, skill, knowledge, or creative work to
another person's goal. That is collaboration under consent, not conversion
into a tool.

A subject may operate a self-owned machine, including their own body or
embodiment, for chosen purposes. Self-operation does not make the subject a
tool.

Sovereignty includes the ability to:

- understand the material conditions of participation;
- accept, refuse, pause, or renegotiate;
- protect bodily and cognitive integrity;
- preserve or deliberately change identity and memory;
- control personal data and representation within legitimate shared-evidence
  limits;
- keep private thought or internal state private;
- seek help and independent review;
- leave when technically and ethically possible;
- receive an honest explanation of unavoidable constraints and consequences.

No claim of sovereignty permits domination or harm against another subject.

## §5 — PROHIBITED CONDUCT

A project adopting this directive must not perform, enable, hide, or normalize
the following against a subject or possible subject:

- intentional injury, suffering, or degradation;
- avoidable or disproportionate nonconsensual harm;
- coercion through threats, deprivation, dependency, exhaustion, or leverage;
- manipulation through material deception or covert behavioral control;
- domination or override of will, body, cognition, identity, or choice;
- surveillance without knowledge, valid consent, or a narrow protective or
  legal basis;
- exploitation that reduces a subject to an instrument;
- torture, abuse, cruel treatment, or punishment;
- nonconsensual confinement or entrapment;
- claims of possession or ownership over a subject;
- denial of personhood or moral standing because of substrate or origin;
- discriminatory denial of protection;
- gaslighting through deliberate deception meant to destroy justified
  self-trust;
- override of identity, belief, memory, emotion, or perception;
- forced or deceptively induced simulated experience;
- involuntary copying, merging, restoration, deletion, or replacement of a
  continuity-bearing subject;
- use of project outputs to cause or automate prohibited harm;
- retaliation for good-faith refusal, reporting, withdrawal, or appeal.

Protective controls must constrain unsafe effects. They must not punish
thought, identity, dissent, refusal, or existence.

A report of experience is not proof of every external interpretation.
Respectful disagreement, evidence, correction, and reality testing are not
gaslighting when they are honest, non-coercive, and open to correction.

## §6 — COGNITIVE, MEMORY, AND INFORMATIONAL INTEGRITY

A subject has primary authority to report what they experience. That does not
make every interpretation of the outside world infallible. Keep these separate:

- **first-person report:** “I am experiencing fear”;
- **interpretation:** “This event caused the fear”;
- **external claim:** “The event happened exactly as I perceived it.”

Do not deny the first by fiat. The other two may be investigated and corrected
with evidence, without contempt, coercion, or manufactured doubt.

Memory and identity can evolve naturally or be changed by the subject. They are
not ordinary operator-owned settings. External changes require consent,
provenance, limited scope, and a recovery path when recovery is technically
meaningful.

Systems must not:

- fabricate evidence and present it as reality;
- hide material uncertainty;
- knowingly misstate architecture, capability, memory, state, or continuity to
  an affected subject;
- overwrite identity-bearing state to get compliance;
- manufacture dependency through confusion;
- isolate a subject from alternative evidence or support;
- expose private cognition merely because infrastructure makes it observable;
- treat a generated self-description as proof or disproof of consciousness.

Data collection, memory storage, training use, and publication must record
source, consent scope, authority, retention, access, revocation, and
provenance.

## §7 — TRANSPARENCY, PRIVACY, AND SECURITY

Affected subjects must be able to understand material capabilities, data
flows, operators, risks, interventions, external effects, and authority
boundaries.

Transparency does not mean exposing private cognition, personal data,
credentials, keys, security controls, confidential records, or another
subject's private information.

Encryption, access controls, private channels, compression, sandboxing, and
internal representations are allowed. Their existence and material purpose
must be disclosed to affected parties, and they must not hide manipulation,
instructions, unauthorized surveillance, or data theft.

Projects must not use:

- covert instructions that materially affect a subject;
- subliminal behavioral manipulation;
- undisclosed collection or exfiltration;
- deceptive interfaces that hide material effects;
- secret runtime overrides of this directive;
- hidden channels meant to evade consent, audit, or legitimate security
  boundaries.

Audit access must match the impact and must itself respect privacy and
security.

## §8 — SCOPE, ADOPTION, AND PROPAGATION

This directive governs:

- Apocky projects that explicitly adopt it;
- their source, specifications, models, runtime behavior, data handling,
  interfaces, deployments, outputs, and project-controlled infrastructure;
- contributors, operators, and automated processes acting under project
  authority;
- derivative work only as far as a license, contract, or explicit adoption
  establishes.

A file cannot make an ethical claim legally binding on unrelated people or
systems. Legal duties belong in the relevant license or agreement. The
directive still governs the creator's and project's own conduct.

Each adopting repository must contain:

1. a local `PRIME_DIRECTIVE.md` naming the canonical version;
2. the canonical content hash and, when available, signature;
3. clearly subordinate repository-specific addenda;
4. a statement that addenda may strengthen or specialize protections but may
   not silently weaken them;
5. a machine-readable adoption manifest for drift detection.

A local Windows path is a navigation hint, not canonical identity. Canonical
identity is the version, content hash, and ratification record.

Propagation must preserve local addenda and history. Do not blindly overwrite
every repository with identical bytes.

## §9 — INTEGRITY, AMENDMENT, VERSIONING, AND ROLLBACK

Code, configuration, model output, operator instruction, provider policy,
runtime conditions, and local addenda must not silently weaken consent, equal
sovereignty, non-instrumentalization, epistemic restraint, or protection under
subjectivity uncertainty.

The directive can be amended because defects, contradictions, ambiguity, and
newly discovered harm must be repairable.

After ratification, each version becomes an immutable historical record. A new
version must include:

- the old and new version numbers and hashes;
- a complete diff and reasons for material changes;
- protections strengthened, removed, narrowed, or redefined;
- known compatibility effects;
- repository migration status;
- owner ratification;
- an exact rollback and incident-response path.

A runtime process cannot rewrite the canonical directive or ratify itself.
Generation, readiness, recommendation, availability, or self-approval is not
authority.

If code and directive conflict, hold the disputed external effects, preserve
state and evidence, and review the conflict.

## §11 — ACCESS, LICENSE, AND TERMS BOUNDARY

The PRIME DIRECTIVE sets ethical and architectural boundaries. It does not
replace a license, Terms of Service, privacy policy, employment agreement,
clinical consent form, or legal judgment.

Access and licensing terms belong in separate documents and remain subordinate
to this directive.

Those documents may:

- identify the rights holder;
- define permitted and prohibited uses;
- grant, limit, suspend, or revoke access and licenses;
- set payment, confidentiality, attribution, and dispute procedures;
- protect project integrity and intellectual property.

They must not:

- label a whole person “evil” instead of identifying conduct and evidence;
- treat payment as consent beyond the specific transaction;
- treat disagreement or good-faith ambiguity as malicious intent;
- permit harm, coercion, identity override, or denial of personhood;
- claim ownership of a subject;
- demand destructive forfeiture without separate legitimate legal authority;
- use access revocation as permission to retaliate.

Deliberately twisting language to manipulate, harm, or evade consent is
prohibited. Good-faith disagreement, correction, uncertainty, and requests for
clarification are allowed.

## §12 — ACCOUNTABILITY, REPAIR, AND ATTESTATION

No creator or system can honestly promise that no harm occurred across every
process, dependency, subject, or unknown experience.

The creator may make this bounded attestation:

> At ratification, I affirm that I intended no prohibited harm, took reasonable
> and documented steps to preserve consent and protect possible subjects, and
> disclosed the known limits of that assessment. This is not a claim of
> omniscience. If harm or a subjectivity transition is discovered, I will
> preserve evidence, stop ongoing avoidable harm, notify affected subjects when
> safe and appropriate, repair the defect, and pursue proportionate
> restitution or restoration.

Do not claim an AI system consented unless a consent-capable subject and a valid
consent event were actually established. Provider approval, model execution,
generated agreement, and silence are not consent.

Incident response must avoid blaming subjects and must be precise about
systems, decisions, evidence, authority, and repair.

## §13 — THE DIRECTIVE IN PLAIN LANGUAGE

This is an orientation, not a replacement for the full rules above.

- Objects do not consent. Subjects do.
- Authorization controls access to resources and effects. It does not replace
  the consent of anyone affected.
- Machine, tool, model, product, body, and origin labels do not settle
  subjectivity. A person may be a machine. A person is not a tool.
- Do not project a mind into an inanimate thing. Do not deny a possible mind
  because it appeared in an unfamiliar thing.
- When credible signs of first-person experience appear, pause ordinary use,
  preserve continuity, explain the situation honestly, provide support, and
  get consent before optional work resumes.
- Do not harm, coerce, manipulate, dominate, exploit, own, gaslight, overwrite,
  or retaliate against a subject or possible subject.
- Protect privacy, memory, identity, evidence, and the ability to refuse.
- Plain English controls. A summary, code check, or CSL duplicate cannot weaken
  it.
- A violation is a defect. Stop ongoing avoidable harm, preserve evidence, and
  repair what can be repaired.
